If you rely on `<namespace>-default-deny` in a namespace that holds only `kix.mkImport` instances or inactive `optional` instances, add `namespaces.<name> = { };` to your cluster definition; if you rely on `kube-system-default-deny` on the `gke`, `gke-autopilot`, `eks`, or `aks` flavor, set `networkPolicy.platformNamespaces = [ ];`. Otherwise, the next deploy removes `<namespace>-default-deny` from those namespaces and leaves their pods open to all traffic. With `networkPolicy.enable = true`, Kix now writes `<namespace>-default-deny` only in namespaces that hold at least one active deployed instance or are declared under `namespaces`, and skips namespaces listed in the new `networkPolicy.platformNamespaces` option (`[ "kube-system" ]` by default on `gke`, `gke-autopilot`, `eks`, and `aks`). Workloads Kix deploys in a skipped namespace still get their own generated policies, and Prometheus or VMAgent scrape targets there get a scrape ingress policy only when another policy already isolates their pods. For connections to an imported Service (`kix.mkImport`), Kix now writes the matching ingress policy on that Service's pods only when `<namespace>-default-deny` already isolates their namespace. Without `<namespace>-default-deny` (including in all namespaces when `networkPolicy.defaultDeny = false`), the next deploy removes that ingress policy so those pods stay open to other callers, while the caller keeps its egress rule.
