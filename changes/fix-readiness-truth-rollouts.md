When a Deployment, StatefulSet, or DaemonSet is not ready, `kix deploy`, `kix rollback`, and `kix status` now inspect its pods to report how many pods on the new revision are ready and why the first unready pod is waiting, such as a scheduling error or a readiness probe that has not passed. Previously, a Deployment stuck on a new pod could report `2/2 ready, 2/2 updated, 1 old pod left` because Kubernetes includes old pods in the ready count. When an unready StatefulSet pod is still on an older revision, all three commands also name the pod and print the `kubectl delete pod` command to replace it if it does not become ready. With the default `OrderedReady` pod management policy, Kubernetes does not replace that pod on its own, even after you roll the StatefulSet back to a working version.
