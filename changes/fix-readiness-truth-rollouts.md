`kix deploy`, `kix rollback`, and `kix status` now describe a Deployment, StatefulSet, or DaemonSet that is not ready in the same words, read from its pods: how many pods of the new revision are ready, and why the first unready pod is waiting, such as a scheduling error or a readiness probe that has not passed. Previously, a Deployment stuck on a new pod could report `2/2 ready, 2/2 updated, 1 old pod left` because Kubernetes includes old pods in the ready count. When an unready pod of a StatefulSet is still on an older revision, the message names the pod from the first check and prints the `kubectl delete pod` command to replace it. With the default `OrderedReady` pod management policy, Kubernetes does not replace that pod on its own, even after you roll the StatefulSet back to a working version.
