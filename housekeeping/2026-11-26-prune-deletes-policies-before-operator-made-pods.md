# 2026-11-26 · `cli/kix/src/cluster/graph` · Prune deletes the network policies of operator-made pods before those pods are gone.

Kix keeps a policy until the workloads whose pod template it selects are deleted. It only knows pod templates it renders. Pods that an operator creates from a custom resource are not covered. On GKE, emptying a definition with monitoring deleted `prometheus-server-server-egress`, `alertmanager-egress`, `alertmanager-peers` and the Alertmanager scrape-ingress policy about two minutes before the `Prometheus` and `Alertmanager` objects were gone, so those pods ran under the default deny without their allow rules. Nothing failed, because no finalizer there needs that traffic. An operator whose cleanup needs network access would hang. The same gap exists on apply: the pods can start before their policies.
